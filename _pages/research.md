<section class="page-banner">
  <p class="eyebrow eyebrow--dark">Research</p>
  <h2>Research themes translated for collaborators, students, and non-specialists</h2>
  <p>My work sits at the intersection of pharmacoepidemiology, health services research, and real-world evidence. I am interested in how medications are used in practice, what risks and benefits they carry outside trials, and how better evidence can improve care.</p>
</section>

<section class="section-block">
  <div class="research-grid">
    <article class="research-card">
      <h3>Medication safety and aging</h3>
      <p class="research-card__summary">A large share of my work asks how medication use affects older adults, especially when common prescribing decisions may influence cognition, function, or quality of care.</p>
      <p><strong>Methods and data:</strong> pharmacoepidemiology, observational studies, EHR data, medication exposure assessment</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> older adults are often exposed to complex medication regimens, and evidence that reflects routine care can help reduce avoidable harm.</p>
    </article>
    <article class="research-card">
      <h3>Delirium and anticholinergic burden</h3>
      <p class="research-card__summary">My dissertation and related studies focus on whether cumulative exposure to anticholinergic medications increases delirium risk in hospitalized older adults and critically ill patients.</p>
      <p><strong>Methods and data:</strong> cumulative exposure algorithms, EHR medication data, delirium outcomes, systematic review</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> delirium is common, serious, and often difficult to prevent. Better evidence can support safer prescribing and more informed clinical care.</p>
    </article>
    <article class="research-card">
      <h3>Oncology supportive care and disparities</h3>
      <p class="research-card__summary">In my postdoctoral work, I study supportive care medication use and outcomes in older adults with cancer, with a particular interest in socioeconomic disparities.</p>
      <p><strong>Methods and data:</strong> SEER-Medicare, oncology pharmacoepidemiology, health disparities research, causal inference</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> supportive care is central to cancer treatment, but access and use are not always equitable. Understanding those gaps can inform better care.</p>
    </article>
    <article class="research-card">
      <h3>Real-world evidence and causal inference</h3>
      <p class="research-card__summary">I use target trial emulation and related causal inference approaches to make observational research more transparent, more rigorous, and more relevant to real decisions.</p>
      <p><strong>Methods and data:</strong> target trial emulation, study design, comparative effectiveness, fit-for-purpose real-world data assessment</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> decisions about medications rarely wait for perfect evidence. Stronger observational methods help us learn more responsibly from the data we already have.</p>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section-block section-block--accent">

  <p>I am especially drawn to collaborative projects where careful study design, clear communication, and practical relevance matter as much as technical correctness. I enjoy working across disciplinary boundaries and translating difficult ideas into forms that clinicians, researchers, and broader audiences can use.</p>
